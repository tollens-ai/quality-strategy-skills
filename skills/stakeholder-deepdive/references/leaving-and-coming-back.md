# Stakeholder deepdive: leaving a view, and coming back to it

Each of these changes what the view covers, and each is the user's choice, not one you offer.

- **They would rather hear from the stakeholder themselves,** or wait for them, such as "I'd rather
  ask them myself". That is how they will find out: record it, in their words, against the
  questions it covers, and go on with what they can tell you now. Those questions stay open in the
  view.
- **They want to leave the whole view,** because nobody in this conversation can give this
  stakeholder's view now. Don't press them to give it after all. "I don't know" about one thing is
  not that choice: that thing becomes a question and the view goes on. When they have made the
  choice:
  1. Tell them what it means: the view is not taken now; later steps go on without it and keep open
     every decision it could change; and the questions saved now are how the view can be taken up
     again. They can still give
     their own understanding instead.
  2. Write in the profile the questions they now have about this stakeholder, from what the
     conversation so far suggests, each with their way of finding out.
  3. In a strategy, record `stakeholder nobody-here "<stakeholder>"`, then close as the skill says.
- **A view left earlier because nobody here could give it,** which the user now wants to give: in a
  strategy, record `stakeholder can-speak "<stakeholder>"`, and start from the questions saved then.
- **A view they wanted to come back to in full,** as the head of the profile or the progress note
  says: go on without asking how much to cover, because coming back to it is what they asked for.
  If they now say how much to cover, such as "a quick pass", keep to it; the close asks again whether
  they want to come back.
