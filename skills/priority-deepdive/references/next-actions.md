For the step "What to do about it" in [priority-deepdive](../SKILL.md).

## How far the plan goes

Plan only as far as what is known supports, because beyond that the plan rests on guesses.

- **Include a step already known to be worth doing,** even while something it depends on is still
  unknown: one that would answer a question the section holds, or one that will be needed if a
  pending decision goes a certain way. Write the second kind under the outcome it depends on: "if
  they keep the old format: test the upgrade".
- **Leave out a guess at the solution:** a step whose worth depends on a cause nobody has found or
  a result nobody has seen. Plan the action that would find that out, and stop there.
- **A session someone runs,** such as a brainstorm, is one way to do the gathering in a "gather,
  then replan" plan, when the answer you can give now is not good enough for what rests on it. The
  section says what it is for, what it should produce and who runs it; how it is run is up to them.

A way of working meant for agents has to be put where agents read and run things: the project's
own skills, tests, linters and instructions, because a plan they never see changes nothing they do.
The strategy points to it there, and says how it will be kept from growing.

## Choosing what to do

- **Three kinds of work answer different questions.** In the words of Edmund Pringle's guide to
  creating a quality strategy, which this pack's approach comes from: "Testing work removes
  uncertainty about *what is*"; "Stakeholder work removes uncertainty about *what should be*";
  "Fixing work improves *what is* until it matches *what should be*." Name an action by what it will
  find out or change, not by what it looks like.
- **Fixing pays off once you are reasonably sure both of how good it is and of how good it must
  be.** Without the first, the fix may be to the wrong thing; without the second, it may aim at the
  wrong bar. A fix need not wait for unknowns that have nothing to do with it. Finding out and
  fixing are separate actions, because what is learned may show the fix is unnecessary or aimed at
  the wrong cause.
- **What comes first.**
  - What other work depends on, whatever its rating. One thing depends on another only if it
    cannot be done without it; an order someone prefers is not a dependency.
  - Asking someone, early, because the wait for their reply runs alongside everything else.
  - Finding out before fixing, where what is learned could change the decision.
  - The cheap observation or conversation before the tool.
  - A gap already known to do harm can be fixed first.
- **Group what can be answered together:** one conversation with the person who built it may
  settle several questions at once.
- **What is left untouched.** If this work took all the effort available, what important question or
  gap would be left alone? That is the cost of choosing it.
- **The whole cost of anything built** is setting it up, running it, keeping it working, people's
  attention and what it depends on, and it is weighed before choosing a tool.
- **The real test is the release reaching the people it is for:** the step that puts it in their
  hands, and the way what they find gets back to the team. Where the team can use it themselves
  first, against what matters, that shows some things and misses others.
- **There is more than one way to reach a goal.** Changing how the team works, what the agents are
  told or how releases go out may work better than changing the product.
- **A plan can take many forms.** It can start from what others already do well, with finding that
  practice as its first step, rather than working from first principles. It can remove things rather
  than add them. Where it would have the strategy owner review a lot, it can ask them only for what
  they alone can judge: a sample, their marks on a few examples to tune a check, or an agent
  screening everything and them judging what it flags.
- **A fix can break something else,** so a check of its effects belongs with it.
- **The plan can change** as what is learned changes where things stand. In the same guide's words,
  "The plan is a starting point, not a contract."
