# Project context: saving and closing

Save `project-context.md` as [the project context](../../../references/templates/project-context.md)
shows. Before the first save, check who can read the quality folder in the arrangement that
`quality-setup` saved; if there is no saved arrangement, open `quality-setup` first. Outside a whole
strategy, run `comms-prep` before drafting and `comms-review` on the draft, because the user reads
it directly; in a strategy the write-up does that.

- Beside each stakeholder, note whether their view will be the user's own or the user's
  understanding of someone else's, in the user's words where they gave them, such as "I'd have to
  ask him", because the deep dive reads it there rather than asking again. Don't record `stakeholder
  nobody-here` in this step, because going on without a stakeholder's view is a choice the user
  makes when the strategy reaches that stakeholder.
- Keep the concerns raised so far, in the speaker's words with who said them, because the
  priorities step checks its table against them.
- Where they spoke about the pre-read's tests or agent reading, keep what they said and the date, and
  point to the digest rather than copying it, because the later steps read the digest itself.
- Mark which parts are your own interpretation or guess. If a saved context changes, update what
  depends on the changed part too, as [what is true
  now](../../../references/templates/every-document.md#what-is-true-now) says.

Then show them in full each item that is your interpretation or guess, and ask what they would
change, because each remains yours, not theirs, until they answer it. Don't read back their own
answers, which they already know. Don't read back the open questions either, because they need no
agreement; ask only how the user could find out each answer, as [when they do not
know](../../../references/strategy-workspace.md#when-they-do-not-know) says. If they have asked to
stop, don't ask any of this now: those items stay saved, marked as yours, and the question waits in
the progress note to be asked when they come back, because someone who is leaving did not ask for a
new question.

In a strategy, record `release "<the release>"` once they have named the release, and
`stakeholders` with the names in the order to take them once they have given it. Record nothing they
have not given.

Record `step context done` once they have answered each of your interpretations and the context is
what the skill's "done when" describes. A part they answered with "I don't know", recorded as their
question, counts as answered. If you took the quicker way that the skill's first part gives, because
they wanted to move quickly or had too little time, and asked only what the next step needs, then
that is the scope they chose: the step is done when those questions are answered, they have answered
your interpretations, and the rest is recorded as not discussed.

Otherwise, for example when they stop before that, keep the work saved so far and record `step
context started --note "<what remains>"`, naming any interpretation of yours they have not yet
answered, because otherwise a later session would mistake a part nobody reached for one they
answered.

Then close as [stopping and coming back](../../../references/stopping-and-coming-back.md) says,
naming the stakeholders they have given in the order to take them, because the steps that follow
take them one at a time. When this skill runs on its own, record nothing: the closing message says
what was saved and where, and stops there with no next step, because they asked for the context
alone and decide for themselves what follows.
