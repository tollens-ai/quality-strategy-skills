# Prioritise release quality: the table

This takes [prioritise-release-quality](../SKILL.md) from the stakeholders' profiles to the strategy
owner's decisions. Work through its parts in order, because each rests on the one before: the list
must be complete before it is ordered, the ratings are worked out from the order, and only once the
rows are settled can you check whether anything was lost.

Before you first save anything, use the arrangement for the records that `records.mjs where`
printed. If it printed none, open `quality-setup` and come back here after it, because the user has
to know who can read what is about to be saved. Outside a whole strategy, run `comms-prep` before
the first save too, because the user will read what you save; within a strategy, the write-up does
this.

Keep the table in the quality folder's `notes/` as you build it, with the rows you put together from
several profiles and the rows you added marked as yours, so that nothing is lost if the session ends
early. As soon as the step begins, write the strategy's opening, its parts on the project, the
release, who matters and what matters to them, and its end parts, from the project context and the
profiles, as [the template](../../../references/strategy-template.md) shows. They rest on steps the
user has already answered, and writing them now means the strategy can be read before it is
finished. Until the close, the opening says the priorities are still being set and has no bottom
line; don't draft one from the profiles. Write what this release will not do, and the priorities,
only once the strategy owner has answered.

## Lay the views side by side

Each row names the product or part, the kind of quality and the stakeholders it matters to, with
what has been said about how good it must be beside it, saying whose each is, and, where the user
gave any, their words on what comes first for each stakeholder.

- **Take the things that matter from each profile.** They are the user's understanding of that
  stakeholder. Put those that concern the same part and the same kind of quality in one row, with
  each stakeholder's bar and what comes first for them beside it, and say whose each is ("said, for
  the support team"). If a profile holds something you added, bring it in as the user answered it in
  that profile.
- **Keep the profiles' words,** quoted or close, for each row's name and each bar, because a
  fresh session has only the records and a rewording is a change nobody agreed. Where the profiles
  behind a joined row word it differently, the wording you give the row is yours: say so, and keep
  each stakeholder's own words beside their bar.
- **Bring in what has been said about how good each row must be, wherever it was said.** The
  profiles hold each stakeholder's levels, but the strategy owner, or someone else, may also have
  said how good something must be in their own view, in someone else's, in the interview, in the
  project context or earlier in this conversation, sometimes as something they would not release
  without, or as a limit. Put each beside its row in the speaker's words, with whose it is and what
  it was about, such as "for this release" or "for their own use", because the records are kept by
  the step that heard each thing and the table by row, so the joining is yours, and a row shown as
  if nobody had spoken misreports what is known.
- **Something a stakeholder said does not matter to them** needs a row only when it matters to
  another stakeholder or the user wants it kept as something this release does not aim at;
  otherwise it stays in their profile.
- **The same word with different meanings stays in separate rows.** What a user can see when
  something goes wrong and what the team can see in its logs are two questions.
- **Different bars for the same thing stay in one row, both visible,** because that difference is
  what the user decides next.
- **Make one row of what is really one thing, and keep apart what only looks alike.** Two things
  that are almost the same, or overlap so much that working on them separately would repeat the
  work, may be one priority; the strategy owner decides. Two things are not one priority just
  because one check, data set or fix would answer both: what makes them one is what they are, not
  how they would be found out.
- **Split a row, or put rows together,** only as [unpack where the answers
  differ](../../../references/quality-dimensions.md#unpack-where-the-answers-differ) describes.
  Parts of one thing that need different fixes or checks are aspects of one row, not reasons to
  split it. Putting rows together must not hide a difference: where the items you put together have
  different bars, keep each one
  visible in the row.
- **Nothing said yet.** Where nobody has said how good a row must be, or the user could not say it
  for a stakeholder, the row says so, and the question goes beside it.
- **A missing view** is marked in every row its answer could change; if nothing shows which rows
  those are, ask the user. Those rows stay in the list, marked provisional, and you mention the gap
  once for all of them, because it is one fact about the group, and raising it at each row asks the
  same question many times.

Don't show the list yet, and don't spend a turn asking what they would change in it. Show it whole
in the message that asks for their ordering, because nobody can order a list they have not seen
whole, and they can correct a row you put together there. Before that, add to the same list what the
next part finds.

## Look for what no view covers

Add each thing you find to the list as yours, with your reason and whom you think it matters to,
because they asked for the priorities, not for your concerns. Describe it as something that matters
to someone, not as a piece of work to do.

- **What was raised before:** the interview's records, the pre-read's major issues and the concerns
  saved in the project context. An item that is already covered needs no turn of its own. It is
  covered if it is a row or an aspect of one, an open question, something the strategy owner has
  already said this release does not aim at (with their reason), or, if it is about who matters or
  is a fact, recorded in the project context. An item that is not covered goes in as a row of yours.
- **[Obligations that come with the
  facts](../../../references/quality-model.md#obligations-that-come-with-the-facts),** for
  everything the product already does, not only what this release adds. If the project context
  already records a fact with the user's answer about it, don't raise it again. For a new fact, put
  it in the list, with the obligation you think follows from it, as yours. Record it in the project
  context only once the user has heard it, because an obligation recorded before they have heard it
  is only your guess, written up as if it were settled. An obligation to people who are not yet
  stakeholders gets a row saying "no stakeholder yet", and those people go into the project context
  as possible stakeholders.
- **The areas the release covers, and the rest of the project context.**
- **[The kinds of quality](../../../references/quality-dimensions.md),** for a concern that belongs
  to no single stakeholder, checking [who it assumes, people or
  agents](../../../references/quality-dimensions.md#check-human-and-agent-assumptions) and whether
  [how good people believe it
  is](../../../references/quality-dimensions.md#separate-actual-quality-from-what-people-believe)
  needs a row of its own.

A row of yours becomes theirs once the user answers it, either by naming it or with a plain yes to
something they could weigh. Open questions stay
where they are, in the profiles and the project context. So does a tension between views that bears
on some rows: note the tension and those rows beside the list in your notes, so that when you come
to rate those rows you remember to raise it.

## Order them

Ask for the order once every view is in or marked missing. What the user said comes first for each
stakeholder may be partial or missing; that holds nothing up, because it only informs the order, and
the order is made here.

Say first, in a line, that how much a priority matters and how good it must be are different
questions, that they may already have said something about the second, which the list shows, and
that each priority's deep dive takes it further. Then, in one message, show the whole list and ask
how they order the priorities for this release: what matters most compared with the rest, what
matters least, and why. Beside each row show what has been said about how good it must be, with
whose, and what comes first for each stakeholder, even where that is partial, so that they see
their own words attributed to them and can correct any of them there; mark the rows you put
together from several profiles, which they can split again, and your additions with their reasons.
Where they have already said what comes first, in their own view, the interview or this
conversation, show it as what they have said so far and ask what changes for the release as a
whole, because asking afresh would make them say it again.

- If their answer corrects the list, such as rows you put together, take the correction and apply
  their ordering to the corrected list. If their answer only corrects the list, you still need to
  ask for the ordering.
- Take the ordering as they give it, whole or partial, in their words, because how the rows compare
  does not exist until they say it. A row they cannot put anywhere in their order is an "I don't
  know": it stays unrated, with no guess, because a guess pushed out of them would later be treated
  as their decision. Ask how they could find out or decide, as [when they do not
  know](../../../references/strategy-workspace.md#when-they-do-not-know) says.
- If "everything is critical" makes comparison hard, or the list is more than they can weigh at
  once, or they ask for help, offer a way that suits them, such as asking what would change if the
  timeline were halved, or your provisional ordering with your reasoning, for them to say what is
  out of place. Until they adopt it, what you offer is only a what-if or your own reading, and it
  does not by itself lower any requirement. "Could we talk it through?" is a way of deciding they
  chose themselves: have that conversation if they want it. It should end with their order, in their
  words, or with a question described better than before.
- There is no right number of Highs, or of things left out, so don't question how many there are.
- A row whose stakeholder's bar is unknown may still be put in their order and rated; the rating is
  theirs and provisional, with the question beside it. If they say they cannot rate it without that
  answer, it is unrated.
- The release's order is kept with the priorities and never written into a stakeholder's profile,
  because a profile stays as the user left it.

## What this release will not aim at

Once they have ordered, ask once, as an open question, what this release does not aim at, and why.
Knowing what is deliberately not a priority tells people where to stop, and a later question about
extra polish can be answered from it. Where their ordering already named some, show those together
as theirs and ask what else. Propose no list of your own; you may name a row they have not put in
their order.

A non-priority is a kind of quality the release deliberately does not aim at. A feature left for
later belongs in the release's scope in the project context, and "we have no tests" says how things
are now, not that checking is unnecessary. Naming a non-priority does not remove an obligation
either: an obligation that comes with the facts stays, however it is rated. What they name is rated
None, with their reason. Polish beyond what the release needs on a priority they keep, such as speed
past a response time, is not a None: it is a limit in that priority's bar, and its rating stands.

## Read the ratings from their order

Work out High, Medium, Low and None from their ordering, with the meanings [the quality
model](../../../references/quality-model.md#ratings-and-bars) gives. Show the ratings once, as
groups beside their ordering, as your reading of what they said, and ask what they would change,
because later steps use the ratings as shorthand and the user is the one to correct them. Refer to
the rows in their words.

- A row they put somewhere in their order carries your rating until they answer it; a row they
  could not put anywhere gets no rating and no guess.
- Rows that a missing or unknown view could change are provisional until that view is known: say so
  once for all of them, naming whose view it is.
- A row needs its own answer when stakeholders' bars or stated priorities conflict on it, or when a
  stakeholder's stated priority goes against the user's order, because you have no single reading
  to show for it. So does a row that a tension you noted bears on: raise the tension with that row
  and ask how it should be settled for this release.
- When a level and a rating seem to clash, first ask what each one is about, because they may
  concern different parts. "They're just ughs" gives a level, not a rating: ask whose view that is,
  then, separately, how much it matters.

A reply covers the rows it addresses. "The rest are fine" covers the rows they were shown, not one
they set aside, such as one they said to leave for later: that row stays unrated with its question,
and you don't press for it now, because setting it aside was their answer; say in a clause that it
stays open for them to take up whenever they choose.

Once the ratings are settled, name each row rated Low or None on which a stakeholder has a
dealbreaker, say whose it is, and ask whether the rating stands, because a dealbreaker rated low
without anyone remarking on it is the difference they would most want to have weighed. Leave out a
row whose rating their order or reasons already explain. If they leave a disagreement between
stakeholders open, it stays an open question until they have found out more; don't force it into
agreement.

Record each rating with its reason. Where a reason was already given, in what they said about the
order or in a profile, carry it, saying whose, because a reason that exists and is left out reads as
missing. Leave the reason empty for a rating given without one, because writing that none was given
reads as if they were asked and had nothing to say. A row they reject is rated None, with their
reason.

## Check that nothing was lost

Only now are the rows settled enough to check against, so go through what was raised before (the
interview's records, the pre-read's major issues and the context's concerns), each stakeholder's
things that matter, the areas the release covers and the obligations that apply, and check that
each is covered:

- **Raised before:** covered if it is a row or an aspect of one, an open question with why it
  matters, something the strategy owner said is not a priority (with their reason), or, if it is
  about who matters or is a fact, recorded in the project context. Don't ask again about one already
  answered earlier in this step.
- **From a stakeholder, the areas or the obligations:** covered if it is a row. The exception is a
  concern the user said should stay in a stakeholder's view: that is covered if their profile holds
  it with a bar.

Tell the user, in one question, which items are not covered, and ask what they want to do about
each. Things a stakeholder said do not matter to them, which were left in their profile, need only
one line for all of them.
