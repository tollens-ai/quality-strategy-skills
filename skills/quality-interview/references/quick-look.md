# A quick look at named files

**Goal.** Use a project fact to help the user develop a concern they have already raised.

**Done when.** What you found has been brought back into the conversation, with what it cannot
show, or you have respected their no or their not yet.

Propose a look only after the first round of broad questions, when an open topic would gain from a
few named project facts that the user would otherwise have to explain. A look the user asks for can
come any time after the first question. A file can show what it says or does; it can't tell you what
the user values, or confirm your reading for them.

**Asking first.** A look the user asked for needs no permission. For one you propose, ask one
unnumbered question naming what you want to look at and how it would help this topic. Say a no is
fine, and offer to make similar looks for the rest of the sitting without asking each time. A yes covers this
look only, unless they say it covers more; then note that choice and its turn in `notes.md`. A no,
or a change of mind, replaces earlier permission.

**The look.** Before you start, note in `notes.md` what you will look at and why. Then read the
named files yourself, or have a subagent read only those and report to you, in the same turn, what
it saw and where. Afterwards note what you read and whether you finished, so a return uses what you
found instead of looking again.

**Bringing it back.** Make the next question about how what you found bears on their concern,
before anything else the look turned up; [choosing the next question](select-next-question.md)
says how to choose it. Say what you looked at and where, and tell apart what you saw from what you
infer. Let the user correct your reading or disagree about what it means: agreeing to the look was
not agreeing with your conclusion.
