# Open questions

These are things we are unsure about and can only answer from real use. If you have tried the
pack, what you saw would help: please
[tell us](https://github.com/tollens-ai/quality-strategy-skills/issues) what you were doing, what
the skill did, and how that helped or got in the way.

- **Whose strategy it is.** When an agent drafts something and the person says "yes", how much of
  it is theirs? How short does a proposal need to be for a yes to mean anything?
- **Length.** A whole strategy is a long conversation, and every check adds a point where the
  agent stops for the person. Which of those stops are worth it?
- **What the person learns.** The most useful moments are when the conversation shows someone a
  consequence they had not seen. How can there be more of those per hour?
- **Agents.** Agents differ in how closely they keep to the steps. What can the instructions do
  about that, and what has to be said plainly as a limit?
- **Using one part alone.** Can a person get something useful from one skill, such as a single
  stakeholder's view, without being drawn into the whole sequence?
- **A second release.** When the next release comes, is it clear what carries over from the last
  strategy and what needs thinking about again?
- **Larger projects.** Does the table of priorities stay readable when there are many of them?
- **The interview.** When is the open interview worth its time, and when is it better to go
  straight to a strategy step?
- **Products whose results vary.** How do you judge quality when the same input gives different
  results, as with AI features?
- **Reports.** Does a report made for one reader keep what is unknown, and where stakeholders
  differ, while still making that reader's next decision easier?
