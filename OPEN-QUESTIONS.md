# Open design questions

This page is written for people. The skills carry their own instructions and do not depend on this page.

These questions need experience from using the skills, beyond paper review.

- **Whose strategy it is:** when an agent drafts something and the person says "yes", how much of
  it is theirs? How short does a proposal need to be for a yes to mean anything?
- **Length:** a whole strategy is a long conversation, and each safeguard adds a point where it
  stops for the person. Which stops earn their place?
- **What the person learns:** the most useful moments are when the conversation shows someone a
  consequence they had not seen. How can there be more of those per hour?
- **Agents:** agents differ in how closely they keep to the steps. What can the instructions do
  about that, and what has to be said plainly as a limit?
- **Module boundaries:** can a person get useful context, one stakeholder perspective, priorities,
  an assessment, or a plan without being drawn into the whole sequence?
- **Release reuse:** are shared profiles and the recorded basis of each release clear enough when
  expectations change? Can users see what needs reconsideration?
- **Document size:** does the priorities table stay easy to read on a larger project? Which
  grouping choices actually help its readers?
- **Existing interview:** when is the open-ended conversation worth its longer recording flow,
  and when should a user enter a focused module directly?
- **Evidence for variable systems:** which judging methods are useful for products whose outputs
  vary, and how should their limits inform decisions?
- **Reports:** do focused reports preserve uncertainty and stakeholder differences while making
  the next decision easier?

Useful feedback describes the situation, what the skill did, and how that helped or hindered the
work. A missing answer is evidence to investigate, not a reason to add a universal requirement.
