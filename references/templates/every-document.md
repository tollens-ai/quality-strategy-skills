# What every document shares

The templates beside this file show what each document holds, and [the strategy
template](../strategy-template.md) shows the strategy's. Anything else worth keeping goes in the
quality folder's `notes/`, and a note for the next session goes in the progress note.

- **Write for someone who wasn't there.** Say which project and release the document is about, when
  it was last updated and where its sources are, and explain project, release and technical terms
  the first time.
- **Call people by their names or job titles,** as suits the person and the sentence, such as their
  first name or "the founder", or "they", and not "the user", because the person often reads the
  document about their own project, and one that calls them "the user" reads as written about them
  to someone else. Once a document has said who the strategy owner is, "the owner" is fine where the
  role is what the sentence is about.

## Whose words are whose

- **Make ownership clear.** Say whether each decision, rating or reading is the user's decision, a
  stakeholder's account, what the project's evidence shows, or the agent's interpretation or guess.
  What the project is for, what matters and how good it must be are the user's to say.
- **Record agreement faithfully.** Something is agreed only when the user actually answered it. A
  reply that names an item answers it. A general yes, such as "yeah, those look fine", to a small set
  they were shown in full adopts the set, and each item keeps a short note that the agent drafted it
  and they let it stand, because a reader should still be able to tell whose words it was in.
  Silence, or "fine" to more than they could weigh, adopts nothing, and the items stay the agent's
  proposals. Judge by common sense whether a reply adopts something, from what they could have
  taken in. Keep their qualifications and who said what, and quote the words a
  consequential decision rests on, with the date. Where they answered by number, from a numbered
  list, write the items' names in place of the numbers and say that you have, because the numbers
  mean nothing away from the list they answered.
- **Keep disagreements visible.** Accounts that conflict go side by side for the user to judge.
  Their decision settles what the strategy does; it does not mean everyone agrees.

## Open stays open

- **Describe unfinished knowledge honestly.** Write what is not known, or what no record supports,
  as a question rather than a guess, and say whether it was not asked or asked and nobody knew. Say
  why it matters and what could resolve it. Every question goes to the user to take forward,
  including one about a stakeholder.
- **Make conditional choices explicit.** Record a risk the user knowingly chose to accept, a
  decision they put off and what would trigger it, and what would reopen either. A chosen approach
  is unproven until it is checked.

## What is true now

- **Keep one current account.** When something changes, update it wherever it is stated and in the
  conclusions that rest on it, because a reader with two answers acts on the wrong one. Describe
  each thing once and link to it elsewhere. History goes in the quality folder's `decision-log.md`,
  only ever added to: a few lines per change that mattered, with the date, what it said and says
  now, and whose change it is. Where the agent has permission to commit, commit before replacing
  a whole document, so that git keeps what it replaces.
- **Preserve the meaning of corrections.** Tell apart an error in the record, a change in the facts
  or in someone's mind, and a new release, which gets its own strategy. Correct the current
  account, and keep a quotation that was really said as it was said.
- **Stay within the agreed work.** When the user asks for a piece of work, that is permission to
  save what it produces. Propose a change nobody asked for before making it, and say in the progress
  note when a change affects work outside what you were asked to do.
- **Keep evidence checkable.** For anything the agent ran, keep what it would take to run it again
  and what it showed in `notes/evidence/<slug>/`, linked from the finding. A fix reported is not a
  problem shown resolved.
