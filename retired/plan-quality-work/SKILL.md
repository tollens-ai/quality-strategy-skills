# Plan quality work

Quality is value to someone who matters. The approach to quality strategy in this pack derives from
the Context Driven Testing school: start by describing who matters, describe what matters to them,
then prioritise the work to achieve it. The approach is not dogmatic - coverage, checklists,
standards and established practice can all be part of the strategy, sometimes they are the exact
right thing for some stakeholder or some quality dimension. The key thing is the overall approach -
based on context, and understanding what this project needs to achieve.

This answers the last of the [four questions](../../references/quality-model.md#four-questions):
what do we do about it. It weighs what stakeholders stand to gain or lose, what is already known,
and what the team can realistically do. A stakeholder is anyone, or any group, whose view of value
matters here.

It rests on knowing what good looks like and how good things are now. Where those are missing,
finding them out is the work. So this step can start from an incident or a concern with no
assessment behind it: the plan then begins with finding out.

After an assessment, it waits until the user has seen the findings and replied, because a plan is
only as good as the findings it rests on.

## What good looks like

Check the work against these as it goes, and again before saving.

### A good result

A good result is the user's next actions, in order: a plan they could start on next week. It is
not a schedule of work for others or a list of whom to ask, because the strategy is theirs.

Each piece is an action of theirs, named by what it is, and says:

- why it is worth doing, and every priority or unknown it serves;
- what it should change in where things stand once it is done;
- who does it: the user, someone they hand it to, in their words, or the agent, with their leave,
  because a request for a plan is not leave to carry it out;
- roughly how much of the time available it takes.

The plan also:

- picks out the first block, fitted to the limit the records hold, and names the pieces the
  release waits for;
- ends each open question that matters in one of the three ways [the plan of
  work](../../references/writing-the-strategy.md#the-usual-shape) gives: an action to find it out,
  a wait the user chose, or no settled way yet, saying what the records hold and what rests on it;
- says, with why, what it puts later and does not do: work left out, the waits and the questions
  with no settled way, beside the rows no piece serves, named with their ratings, because a plan
  that hides a question it cannot act on is the worse plan.

If nothing useful can be chosen yet, the result is the clearer problem or question, saved with why
it matters and marked as still open.

### A good conversation

The agent asks what has been tried, and about the limits the records don't give. The plan reaches
the user as a set they can weigh, as [put the plan to the user](references/put-the-plan.md) says.

### A good record of agreement

The record keeps the agent's proposals apart from what the user chose, and holds nothing they did
not see, as [put the plan to the user](references/put-the-plan.md#the-record-of-agreement) says.

### Done when

The user has replied to the plan, or to the clearer question, and the plan notes and the strategy's
plan of work read as above.

## Every turn

Before your first message, open the page for the tool you are running in,
[Codex](../../references/codex.md) or [Claude Code](../../references/claude-code.md), because it
says how to ask there so that the answer is waited for. Another tool has no page.

These hold for every message in this step. [Each
turn](../../references/strategy-workspace.md#each-turn) gives the reasons and the rest: open it
when a turn will take over a minute, or they are short of time or tiring.

- **It is their strategy:** suggest freely, decide nothing, and give them an amount they can weigh.
- **One message a turn,** after the last tool call. Say what is new to them, not their answer back
  or that it is recorded, and ask **one question at most,** which ends the turn: one question put to
  a set of items is one; two different questions are two.
- **Open questions,** answered in their own words. A go-ahead, a plain fact or whether a proposal
  shown in full stands can be asked directly.
- **Their project, not the agent's process.**
- **Do what the skill asks;** if something looks worth leaving out, say what the later work would
  lack and let them decide.
- **Thinking comes first:** explain an assumption, a tension or an insight that could change the
  work, and let them explore it or park it. Examine what you are given, and keep the rest of what
  you notice as [the Open guidance](../../references/open-section.md#what-you-notice) says.
- **Unknown is not unasked,** and **say only what happened.**

Open these when their moment comes, on any turn:

- [drafting steps without the user](../../skills/draft-strategy-steps/SKILL.md): they ask to leave steps,
  or the rest of this one, to the agent;
- [before a new step, a longer look or a
  check](../../references/look-and-check.md): before anything they have not
  asked for, or a change to the product;
- [help them think first](../../references/help-them-think-first.md): they park
  a question, or an unknown stops a choice.

## 1. Start from findings the user has seen

**Goal.** Rest the plan on what is known and unknown, as the user has seen it.

**Done when.** The agent knows what the plan starts from, and the user has replied to any findings
it rests on.

Open [start and look again](references/start-and-look-again.md) and follow its first part, which
finds what the plan starts from.

## 2. Look at the ratings and bars again

**Goal.** Have the user decide what to do where the gaps, now known, put a bar out of reach or
make a rating no longer fit.

**Done when.** Each such rating and bar has been put to the user, and each change they made is
recorded with the earlier value kept.

Follow the second part of that file.

## 3. Choose the work and its order

**Goal.** Work out which actions are worth doing, what each is for, and in what order.

**Done when.** Each action is explained, the order has its reasons, and what the plan leaves out
is said.

How to get there is for the agent and the user to work out. Open [choose the
work](references/choose-the-work.md): ideas to use while choosing, then what to do in order.

## 4. Put the plan to the user

**Goal.** Have the user see the proposals and their reasons, and choose.

**Done when.** The user has replied to the proposals, their order and their trade-offs, or to the
clearer question when no plan emerged.

Open [put the plan to the user](references/put-the-plan.md) before drafting, because it says how
the draft is checked and put to them.

## 5. Save and close

**Goal.** Leave a plan a later session can act on, with what was agreed kept apart from what was
only proposed.

Once the user has replied, or when they stop before then, open [save and
close](references/save-and-close.md) and follow it, because it says where the plan goes and how the
step is recorded.
