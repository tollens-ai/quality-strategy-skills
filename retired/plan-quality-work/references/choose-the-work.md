# Plan quality work: choose the work and its order

These help while choosing and ordering; none is a step, so use each where it fits:

- **Ask what has been tried,** because what to do next depends on that history.
- **Ask about limits the records don't give,** such as time, people or how much risk the user
  accepts, and how finished work reaches the people who use it if the answer would change the
  plan. Ask what important unknown or gap would go untouched if this work took the effort
  available.
- **Three kinds of work answer different questions.** In the words of Edmund Pringle's guide to
  creating a quality strategy, which this pack's approach comes from, and where "the risk map"
  means the assessment of required against actual quality:

  > - **Testing work** removes uncertainty about *what is*. It investigates the current state of
  >   quality on a dimension and upgrades your confidence in the "actual" column of the risk map.
  > - **Stakeholder work** removes uncertainty about *what should be*. It involves talking to
  >   stakeholders, observing users, reviewing market expectations — anything that upgrades your
  >   confidence in the "required" column of the risk map.
  > - **Fixing work** improves *what is* until it matches *what should be*. It closes the gap
  >   between actual and required quality on a dimension.

  Name the kind by what the action will find out or change, not by what it looks like. Other work
  can help too, such as working out how to judge a result or changing how the team works. None has
  a quota.
- **Fixing pays off once both sides are reasonably sure.** Without knowing how good it is, the fix
  may be to the wrong thing; without knowing how good it must be, it may be to the wrong bar. A
  fix need not wait for unrelated unknowns.
- **Keep finding out and fixing as separate actions,** because what is learned may show the fix is
  unnecessary or aimed at the wrong cause.
- **What comes first.**
  - Work that other work depends on comes first, whatever its rating. Say what shows the
    dependency. One piece depends on another only if it cannot be done without it. An order
    someone prefers is not a dependency, because wanted work that is called a prerequisite gets
    scheduled as compulsory.
  - Where the user's way of finding something out is to ask someone, that action of theirs comes
    early, alongside whatever else starts. The reply takes that person's time and the plan cannot
    shorten it: asked first, the wait runs under the other work instead of after it.
  - Find out before fixing when what is learned could change the decision. An unknown on a
    high-stakes question can hide a large gap, so it often comes before a smaller known one.
  - Put any checkpoint the user asked for straight after the finding out, before the costly work
    it could change.
  - When the evidence already shows a harmful gap, fixing it can come first.
  - Ratings inform the order without deciding it, for Medium, Low and unrated rows alike, and no
    rating keeps a necessary action out. A Low row that must still work is not optional
    polish, and can hold something that stops the release, as
    [importance](../../../references/quality-model.md#importance) describes. A what-if recorded with
    the priorities, such as "I'd drop that if the timeline halved", changes no rating and drops no
    work, because it was recorded as a what-if for that reason.
- **The plan reaches the people the release is for.** Name the step that puts the release in
  their hands, and how what they find comes back into the strategy, because that step is the real
  test of the release. Choose who sees it first by who can put up with failures, who can give the
  evidence needed, and what getting it wrong would cost.
- **If the plan goes straight from building to those people,** first check whether the team can
  use the product themselves against the bars that matter. Say what that shows and what it misses
  of those people's circumstances. If the team's own use isn't practical, say what would work
  instead where the agent can see something, as its suggestion, or that the records hold nothing
  that would, because assuming there is an alternative invents one.
- **Try the cheap thing first.** An observation or a conversation that can answer the question
  comes before a tool. Count the whole cost of anything built: setup, running, upkeep, people's
  attention and what it depends on.
- **There is more than one way to reach a goal.** Changing who reviews what, what the agents are
  told, a handover or how releases go out may work better than changing the product. Say what
  would change, what it should achieve and the evidence or assumption behind that. Record it as
  planned work; the project context keeps the current way of working until the team takes the
  change up.
- **A fix can break something else,** so plan a check of its effects. To judge where problems are
  likely, look at what is new, changed, complex or poorly understood, as well as the harm a
  problem would do. Show the reasoning without inventing numbers.
- **To rank options against criteria the user has chosen,** open [priority
  analysis](../../priority-analysis/SKILL.md) for the ranking and take it back, as [when one skill
  opens another](../../../references/opening-another-skill.md) says.
- **The plan can change.** In the same guide's words:

  > **The plan of work is not immutable.** As you execute the plan and learn things, the risk map
  > updates, and the plan of work may need to change. That's not a failure of planning — it's the
  > strategy working as intended. The plan is a starting point, not a contract.

Then, in this order:

1. Outside a whole strategy, run `comms-prep` now, because it has to come before the first draft of
   a record the user will read. In its brief, say what the record is for as [each
   turn](../../../references/strategy-workspace.md#each-turn) says under "What the records are for", because the review works from the
   brief. In a whole strategy the connecting step does this once, at the end.
2. Take in, as work here, what the records already ask for, as well as the gaps the findings show,
   because a wish on record that the plan drops without saying so is a decision nobody made:
   - the measuring that was too costly for the measuring step: the rows the measurement plan marks
     `work for the plan of work`, and the checks the Open section holds;
   - the work the user has asked for in the records: a limit or wish in the project context, a
     Delight in a profile, an entry in the Open section.
3. Find each open question that matters here whose way of finding out is "not yet worked out", in
   the Open notes or the assessment. A question matters here when something in the plan rests on
   it: a row the release waits for, a High row, the order of the work, or a rating the plan needs.
   The other questions keep "not yet worked out" and are not asked again in this step, because
   nothing in the plan rests on them and the Open notes keep them.

   Ask the user how we could find out, for all of them in one question, before drafting, because
   this is the step that turns a question into work and the way is theirs to choose, as [the Open
   guidance](../../../references/open-section.md#when-the-user-does-not-know) says. "Talk it through
   with my agent" is a reasonable answer: if they want that now, do it now. Where they are stuck,
   offer a way the agent can see, said to be the agent's, because an unknown nobody is pursuing is
   a risk nobody is managing; once is usually enough, because pressing them gets a guess.

   Record what they say in the question's Open entry, in their words, with any way the agent
   offered named as its own and whether they took it up.
4. Explain each action with the parts [the plan
   notes](../../../references/saving-records.md#the-plan-notes) give each piece. Of those:
   - A size is a judgment the agent offers. "Effort unknown" is not an answer where the records
     hold a limit, because a plan with no sizes cannot be fitted to it.
   - The agent does a piece only with their leave, as [a
     check](../../../references/look-and-check.md) requires. A piece with nobody named is the
     user's, and says so, because every piece is one of their next actions.
5. Make one piece of an action that serves several rows, naming every row it serves, and link each
   piece to the ideas and open items it draws on. Two rows with unrelated actions are two pieces,
   whatever their ratings, and nothing groups pieces by rating, because an action on a lower-rated
   row can be one the release needs.
6. Give the order, with the reason for each action's place kept with it. Rows rated None get no
   work.
7. Say what the plan covers:
   - For each piece of work the user asked for in the records, whether it is in or left out, and
     why. Work "not needed yet" goes later in the order and is not left out.
   - For each row the release waits for, whether what it must show is already shown by the
     findings, planned here, or put off because the user said so; or that there is no settled way
     yet to show it, said with what the records hold, as [the plan of
     work](../../../references/writing-the-strategy.md#the-usual-shape) says of an open question,
     and with their word on whether the release still waits for it. Say that nobody yet knows how
     only where they said they know of no way. Having no way is not the same as their choosing to
     put it off.
   - For each open question that matters, its outcome, read from its Open entry as [the plan of
     work](../../../references/writing-the-strategy.md#the-usual-shape) gives: a way of theirs that
     they have not put off is the action, a way they put off is their wait, and a way the agent
     suggested is an action only if they took it up.
   - Where the records hold a limit, such as time, money or a paid month, what fits within it and
     what does not, naming the work that comes first, such as the first week's.
   - What is left out or put later, with why, naming together, with their ratings, the rows no
     piece serves.
