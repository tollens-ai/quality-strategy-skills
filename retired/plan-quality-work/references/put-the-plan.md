# Plan quality work: put the plan to the user

The plan reaches the user as a set they can weigh:

- each piece named as one of their actions, with its reasons, its size and who does it;
- the first block picked out;
- the step that puts the release in people's hands described in the message itself, not only
  named;
- what the plan changes in the strategy's bottom line, where it changes a judgment there, because
  the opening is the first thing the next reader sees and the user can correct only what they were
  shown. A plan that changes no judgment there adds nothing to the message.

They reorder, drop, add or hand pieces to someone in their own words, in one reply. Someone with a
few hours a week knows what their next free evening is for.

1. Draft the plan. If nothing useful emerges, draft the clearer question instead, as [the Open
   section](../../../references/open-section.md) describes, with why it matters.
2. Outside a whole strategy, run `comms-review` on the draft and deal with what it finds.
3. Put the proposals, their order and their trade-offs to the user, as [judgments and
   labels](../../../references/judgments-and-labels.md) describes, and wait for their reply before
   saving anything as the plan.

## The record of agreement

The record keeps the agent's proposals apart from what the user chose after hearing the reasons.
Their reply is quoted once, against the plan as it was put to them. A reorder they asked for is
made, and the notes say they asked. A piece they did not answer stays proposed.

Nothing is in the saved plan that the user did not see. Anything the agent adds afterwards is said,
in words, to be the agent's.

Pieces are named, not numbered, because numbers change when the plan is reordered, and a reply that
pointed at a number then points at the wrong work.
