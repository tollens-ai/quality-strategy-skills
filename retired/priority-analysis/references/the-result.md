# The result and the record

Open this once the evidence and views are set out. It covers working out what the comparison
supports, writing it down and giving it to the decision owner.

## What the comparison supports

A result is a strict order or ordered groups: two or more ranked tiers, with the options within a
group tied. One group holding every option orders nothing. Say three things about it in words,
because together they tell a reader whether they are looking at a decision or a proposal:

- **What the comparison supports:** an order, ordered groups, or no defensible order. Stop
  comparing when going on would mean inventing an input, and say what is missing: criteria you
  proposed that the decision owner has not adopted, a criterion they have not said how to apply,
  adopted criteria that conflict with no rule from them for settling it, missing access or evidence
  that matters, or a stakeholder's input they required that has not arrived. Disagreement among
  background views does not hold the result up.
- **Whether the decision owner adopted it,** or the person they named. Adopting the result adopts
  the criteria it rests on; record that. If they accept it only "for now", record what they said and
  what would reopen it.
- **What is unknown, tied or still needed,** and what it could change.

## The record

Before drafting, run `comms-prep`, with a brief that says what the record is for, unless another
skill opened this one and does that itself. Give the result in the conversation unless the user
names a file or folder for it; a file they gave you as evidence is not that place.

Say at the start whether the result is adopted or proposed, so nobody takes a proposal for an
agreed decision, and explain each position by the criteria, constraints and evidence. A result
resting on criteria you proposed is framed as "if <their name> adopts these criteria".

Write the record at the size the decision needs: the result, its reasons, the evidence it rests on,
and what is still open, with who owns the decision, who said what and the sources given once. A
comparison of two or three options can be a paragraph or a short table in the conversation. For a
larger decision, keep each stakeholder's view apart and mark views the decision owner has not
adopted as background, because a merged view hides whose it was.

## Check it before handing it over

Read the record back. Every option, criterion, weight, constraint, tie-break and position should
trace to a named source, the decision owner's adoption or the evidence; disagreements, unknowns and
ties should still be visible; and it should say plainly whether the result is adopted. Fix what does
not hold, and leave a position you cannot support unresolved.

## Giving it to the decision owner

Run `comms-review` on the finished draft, unless another skill opened this one and does that
itself. When nobody can reply, the written record is the result: stop there.

Otherwise show the comparison and the result, which can come in the same message. If something
holds the result up, keep the comparison short, ask the one question that would unblock it, and end
your turn: for criteria you proposed, what they would decide by; for a missing required input or
evidence, what it could change and what they want to do about it; for adopted criteria that
conflict, how they want to weigh them. If there is no defensible order, ask for a tie-break only if
they need a strict order. Otherwise nothing more is needed from them.

If their reply adopts the result, record that; if it supplies what was missing, go back to where it
was needed. If what holds the result up is an input or evidence they do not have, or would be
guessing at, go on as [when they do not
know](../../../references/strategy-workspace.md#when-they-do-not-know) says, because a comparison
that ends with how the missing thing will be found out is more use to them than one that ends with a
named gap. Put what came of it with what the record holds as unknown or still needed. Then bring the record up to date.
